# CMPUT 229: Computer Organization and Architecture I

## What It Covers

You write RISC-V assembly and run it in the RARS simulator. The [Fall 2026 labs](https://cmput229.github.io/229-labs-RISCV/) build up from an intro to RISC-V and RARS to programs like a typing game, a sorting visualizer, and a perceptron branch predictor. In Fall 2026 the lab assignments themselves are ungraded practice: you're assessed by a one-hour paper-based lab exam in your scheduled lab session, which closely follows the corresponding lab. Doing the labs properly is how you prepare.

## What Students Say

> Imported from the original course reviews page, which mixed student contributions with summarized Reddit discussion. Tips aren't term-stamped yet; if you've taken this course recently, [add your experience](https://github.com/uofa-cs/uofa-cs-wiki/issues/new?template=course-review.yml).

Assembly language, CPU design, memory hierarchy. Consistently cited by students as the hardest course in the program: *"One time a friend asked me, 'How's life besides 229?' I quickly answered, 'There is no life besides 229.'"*

Understanding what happens at the hardware level makes you a better programmer, but the workload (labs plus difficult exams) is punishing.

**Instructor notes:**
- **José Nelson Amaral:** high standards, hard labs and exams, and very energetic lectures. He posts course videos on YouTube (see the [CMPUT 229 channel](https://www.youtube.com/@CMPUT229)) and students in other sections use them too.

**Student tips:**
- Whatever section you're in, the [CMPUT 229 YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@CMPUT229) is a widely recommended supplement.
- Pairs reasonably with 291 (databases), which has lighter workload. **Do not** take 229 with 301 and 350 in the same semester.
- The memory hierarchy concepts in 229 directly feed into 379 (Operating Systems); don't treat it as disconnected content.
