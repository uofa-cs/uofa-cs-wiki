# Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers to the questions that come up every year. Each one links to the page that has the details and sources; if an answer here ever disagrees with that page, the page wins.

## Program and Degree

### Should I do the Major, Honors, or an Option?

Default to the plain Major: it's the most flexible path (54 units of CS requirements) with the lowest GPA floor. Pick Honors if you're aiming at grad school and can hold a 3.0 every Fall/Winter; pick the AI or Software Practice Option only if you want that full sequence guaranteed on your transcript. Details and the full comparison: [Program Overview](../getting-started/program-overview.md#which-should-you-pick).

### I started before Fall 2024. Does any of this apply to me?

You may still be in the older General, Specialization, or Honors programs. The [Faculty of Science regulations](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/content.php?catoid=69&navoid=20749) in the 2026-27 calendar cover those "Pre-Fall 2024" programs; check your [Academic Advisement Report](../getting-started/degree-planning.md#registration-and-your-degree-audit) for what you're actually held to.

### Can I switch into CS from another BSc subject?

Within the BSc, you declare a Major before 60 units, switching between Major and Honors needs an application, and changing subject area normally isn't allowed after 90 units ([Program Overview](../getting-started/program-overview.md#major-vs-honors-the-rules)). Transferring in from another faculty is a separate process; ask CS advising at [csugrad@ualberta.ca](mailto:csugrad@ualberta.ca).

### Can I finish in 3 years?

Only with summer courses, and the summer menu is thin: 174, 175, 272 and most first-year MATH and STAT courses have had Spring/Summer sections, but upper-year CMPUT courses generally haven't, and several run in only one term. The real constraint is the 174 → 272 → 204 chain. See [Degree Planning](../getting-started/degree-planning.md).

### What else does the BSc require besides CS courses?

ENGL/WRS, an Indigenous course, breadth inside and outside Science, a lab/field course, and minimums for Science, senior, and 300/400-level units. The table is in [Degree Planning](../getting-started/degree-planning.md#choosing-electives).

## Courses

### 174/175 or 274/275?

274/275 is the accelerated, studio-style, limited-enrollment version, and the catalogue strongly recommends prior programming. Take it if you've written real programs and want C++ and Linux early; otherwise take 174/175. Both reach 204 at the same time, and 275 replaces 201. See [First Year Guide](../getting-started/first-year-guide.md).

### Which math courses should I take?

For a Major, the usual combo is MATH 144 → 146, MATH 125, and STAT 151, with Calculus I, linear algebra and stats in your first year. MATH 114 doesn't count for any CS path. See [Math and Stats](../getting-started/math-and-stats.md).

### What should I take in first year?

174 (or 274), Calculus I, linear algebra and intro stats in Fall; 175 (or 275), **CMPUT 272**, and Calculus II in Winter. Taking 272 in your first Winter is what lets 204 start in second year. See [First Year Guide](../getting-started/first-year-guide.md).

### Which courses matter most for getting a job?

204 is the algorithms course behind interview-style problems, and 303/403 are contest-style problem solving on Kattis. 301 (Java/Android team project), 401 (group project), and 404 (web apps) are the most industry-shaped. Check the [Curriculum Map](../skills/curriculum-map.md) for what each covers and what the degree doesn't teach at all (frontend frameworks, cloud, system design), and [course pages](../courses/index.md) for how they actually run.

### What languages will I learn?

Python (174/175, 274), C (201), C++ (275, 350, 415), RISC-V assembly (229), SQL (291), Java (301), Lisp and Prolog (325), and Django/JavaScript (404). The [Curriculum Map](../skills/curriculum-map.md) lists each course's tools with sources.

### Is CMPUT 391 still offered?

No scheduled offerings since Winter 2022, so don't plan around it ([Degree Planning](../getting-started/degree-planning.md#prerequisite-map)). The [Curriculum Map](../skills/curriculum-map.md) lists other courses that haven't run recently.

## Internships and Jobs

### Is there a CS co-op?

No. The Science Internship Program (SIP) is it: one paid, full-time block of 4, 8, 12, or 16 months, starting in May, September, or January, and 8+ months earns a "with internship" designation on your transcript. You need 24 units to apply and 48 to start. See [Science Internship Program](../career/science-internship-program.md).

### Is SIP worth it?

If you're in the Software Practice Option it isn't optional (8+ months). Otherwise it's worth it if you want a long placement or employers that only hire through formal internship programs, and you can accept WKEXP fees while you work and the Faculty's warning that it "might add a year" to your degree. A May to August internship found on your own needs none of that. See [SIP vs. a summer internship](../career/science-internship-program.md#sip-vs-finding-a-summer-internship-yourself).

### When should I apply for internships?

The Faculty's [internship timeline](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/science/student-services/internship-careers/student-interns/internship-timeline.html) says apply to SIP at the end of first year and start applying for placements in second year. For summer internships at larger companies, start in the fall before. See [Internships](../career/internships.md).

### Can international students do SIP?

Yes, though some positions require Canadian citizenship. Under IRCC's April 2026 change you no longer need a separate co-op work permit, but conditions apply; confirm with ISVS before accepting an offer. See [SIP: International Students](../career/science-internship-program.md#international-students).

### Is there a tech scene in Edmonton?

A real but small one: a handful of tech companies (Jobber, AltaML, Huawei's research team), plus utilities, ATB, and government. Look nationally and remotely too. See [Edmonton's Tech Scene](../career/edmonton-tech-scene.md).

### How much do software developers make in Edmonton?

Job Bank's median for software developers and programmers in Edmonton is $40.87/hr versus $48.08 in Toronto (all experience levels, not intern or new-grad pay). Rent is also much lower. Numbers and sources: [Edmonton's Tech Scene](../career/edmonton-tech-scene.md#the-money-edmonton-vs-bigger-markets).

### Does my GPA matter?

Some lines are hard: 2.3 to stay in SIP, 3.0 to stay in Honors, 3.5 for Jason Lang and Awards Hub academic scholarships, and a 3.0 minimum (often 3.5+ in practice) for UofA CS grad school. Above those, projects and internships usually matter more for industry jobs. See [Scholarships](scholarships.md) and [Grad School](../research/grad-school.md).

## Research and Grad School

### Can undergrads do research? Does it pay?

Yes. Volunteer, take CMPUT 399/499 for credit, or apply for an NSERC USRA (at least $8,400 for 16 weeks; citizens and PRs) or a URI Stipend ($7,500; no citizenship requirement listed). All need a confirmed supervisor before the February deadline, so start asking in October or November. See [Getting Into Research](../research/getting-into-research.md).

### Who should I email about research?

Pick two or three faculty whose recent papers interest you, from [Research Areas and Groups](../research/ualberta-labs.md), and read their directory profile first. How to write the email: [Getting Into Research](../research/getting-into-research.md#how-to-email-a-professor).

### Should I go to grad school?

Yes if you want research or ML roles where a thesis MSc is increasingly the floor; probably not if you want to be a software engineer. UofA CS admits once a year, you need a supervisor to agree to take you, and funded positions are the norm. See [Grad School](../research/grad-school.md).

## Money

### What's the tuition?

For Canadian citizens and permanent residents, most undergraduate courses have fee index 6: **$729.36 per 3-unit course** in 2026-27, so a 30-unit Fall/Winter year is about $7,300 before non-instructional fees ([Registrar](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/registrar/costs-tuition-fees/undergraduate-tuition/fall-winter-tuition-for-canadian-citizens-and-permanent-residents.html)). International students and other programs differ; use the Registrar's [cost calculator](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/registrar/costs-tuition-fees/index.html).

### What scholarships should I apply for?

Awards Hub every year (February 1 to March 31), plus Jason Lang if you're an Alberta resident with a 3.5 GPA (deadlines September 15 and January 15). Paid research awards are on the same page. See [Scholarships and Awards](scholarships.md).

## Campus and Community

### Where do I get help with a course or my program?

Your lab instructor and TAs for course material; CS advising ([csugrad@ualberta.ca](mailto:csugrad@ualberta.ca)) for program and prerequisite questions; UACS and Ada's Team for tutoring. See [First Year Guide](../getting-started/first-year-guide.md#where-to-get-help).

### How do I get on the department's Linux machines?

SSH to `ohaton.cs.ualberta.ca` with your CCID, or use the UCommons lab machines. On Windows, install WSL. See [Tools and Setup](../skills/tools-and-setup.md).

### Which clubs should I join?

UACS for friends in the program, UAPSPC for competitive programming, UAIS for ML, and a hackathon (HackED Beta in November, HackED in February) to build something with a team. See [Clubs and Organizations](../community/clubs-and-orgs.md) and its events calendar.
