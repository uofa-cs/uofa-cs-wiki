# Edmonton's Tech Scene

Edmonton is a real but small tech market. A handful of local companies hire CS students, the public sector and utilities hire a lot of IT staff, and AI research (Amii, Huawei's lab) punches above the city's weight. Most of the biggest-name internships are elsewhere or remote, so treat Edmonton as one market among several, not your only one.

*Last verified: October 2026.*

---

## The Money: Edmonton vs. Bigger Markets

**Wages are lower here.** Government of Canada Job Bank hourly wages (updated November 19, 2025):

| Occupation | Edmonton median | Alberta median | Toronto median | Vancouver (Lower Mainland) median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Software developers and programmers (NOC 21232)](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/22540) | $40.87 | $48.08 | $48.08 | $52.40 |
| [Software engineers and designers (NOC 21231)](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/5485) | $50.24 | $51.92 | $56.49 | $62.50 |

Edmonton's range is $30.00 to $72.12/hr for developers and $32.50 to $72.55/hr for engineers. These are all experience levels, not new-grad or intern pay. For student pay, see the (planned) community pay survey; until then, don't trust any number nobody can source.

**Rent is much lower.** Average two-bedroom rent in purpose-built apartments, October 2025 ([CMHC Rental Market Report data tables](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-data/data-tables/rental-market/rental-market-report-data-tables), Table 1.0):

| City (CMA) | Avg. 2-bedroom rent | Vacancy |
|---|---|---|
| Edmonton | $1,603 | 3.8% |
| Toronto | $2,046 | 3.0% |
| Vancouver | $2,363 | 3.7% |

These averages include older units; a unit you move into today will cost more, especially in Toronto and Vancouver.

**Our take:** the best financial setup is a remote job paying a national or US salary while you live in Edmonton. That's easier mid-career than as a new grad, when in-person mentorship matters more. Early on, don't rule out moving for a strong first job.
