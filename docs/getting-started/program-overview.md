# BSc CS Program Overview

So you've been admitted to Computer Science at the University of Alberta, or you're thinking about it. Before you register for your first semester, you need to understand what degree you're actually working toward, because the choice you make here has real downstream consequences for your schedule, GPA, and career trajectory.

Here's the honest breakdown.

---

## How the Program Is Structured

Computing Science is a subject area inside the Faculty of Science's 120-unit [Bachelor of Science (Major and Honors)](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110969) degree. On top of the CS courses, every BSc student also has to satisfy the degree's common course requirements (for example, 6 units of ENGL or WRS and a 3-unit Indigenous course) and the science and senior-level course requirements. Your CS subject area is only part of the 120 units.

As of the 2026-27 calendar, the [Computing Science subject area](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110935) comes in these flavours:

| Path | Subject area units | What makes it different |
|------|--------------------|-------------------------|
| Major in Computing Science | 54 | The most flexible path. A core plus open 300/400-level CMPUT choices. |
| Major, Artificial Intelligence Option | 72 | Adds a fixed AI sequence: CMPUT 200, 261, 267, 365, 366, 467, plus listed AI electives. |
| Major, Software Practice Option | 78 | Adds 301, 325, 379, 401, 402, 12 units of Business courses, and a mandatory 8-, 12-, or 16-month Science Internship (SIP). |
| Honors in Computing Science | 72 | More CMPUT depth than the Major, higher performance standard, and a capstone-style requirement. |
| Honors, Artificial Intelligence Option | 90 | Honors version of the AI Option; also requires CMPUT 469 (AI Capstone). |
| Honors, Software Practice Option | 93 | Honors version of Software Practice, SIP included. |
| Minor in Computing Science | 24 | For students majoring in something else. |

Those unit counts are the subject area only, as listed in the calendar. **Always read the [calendar page](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110935) for the exact course lists**; this page summarizes, it does not replace it.

### What every Major and Honors path shares

- **Foundations:** CMPUT 174 and 175 (or 274 and 275), first-year calculus (MATH 134/144/154 followed by MATH 136/146/156; Honors also accepts MATH 117/118), linear algebra (MATH 125 for the Major; MATH 125 or 127 for Honors), and an intro stats course (STAT 151, 235, or 265).
- **CMPUT core:** 204 and 272 everywhere, plus some or all of 201, 229, and 291 depending on the path. The plain Major only requires two of 201/229/291; Honors and the Software Practice Option require all three.
- **Ethics:** Every Major and Honors path requires CMPUT 200 (Responsible Data Science and Artificial Intelligence) or CMPUT 300 (Computers and Society). The AI Option specifically requires CMPUT 200. The Minor does not require either.
- **If you take 275:** you cannot also get credit for 201, and the calendar has you replace 201 with another CMPUT course at the 200-level or above.

### Major vs Honors

The Major is the default and it is completely sufficient for industry. Nobody at Google, Shopify, or a Series A startup is going to reject your resume because you did a Major instead of Honors. What matters is what you built, where you interned, and how you perform in technical interviews.

Honors requires more CMPUT courses (the plain Honors path requires 201, 229, 291, a second stats course, 18 units of 300/400-level CMPUT and 12 units of 400-level CMPUT) and, in the plain Honors path, 3 units from CMPUT 399, 401, 469, or 499. **There is no thesis requirement in the current calendar.** The Faculty describes Honors as including "a research or capstone experience" and demanding "a higher standard of performance" ([source](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110969)).

Be honest with yourself about why you want Honors. If you are interested in grad school or research, it makes sense, and it is a good excuse to get to know faculty. If you are doing it because you think software engineering recruiters care, they mostly don't. They care about your internships, your projects, and whether you can solve problems in an interview.

### The AI Option

If you already know you want ML or AI, the AI Option bakes in the courses you would probably take anyway: 261, 267, 365 (Introduction to Reinforcement Learning), 366, and 467, with a capstone (469) in the Honors version. The trade-off is less freedom for other electives. Check the prerequisites early: 267 has corequisites in 272, linear algebra, and stats, and 365 needs 267, 466, or STAT 265 ([catalogue](https://apps.ualberta.ca/catalogue/course/cmput)).

### The Software Practice Option

This is the industry-oriented path. It requires the software engineering sequence (301, 401, 402), 325 and 379, 12 units of Business courses, and a Science Internship Program placement of 8, 12, or 16 months. The calendar is blunt about the internship: students who fail to complete a SIP placement must withdraw from the option and reapply to continue in the regular Major or Honors ([source](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110935)).

## Faculty of Science, Not Engineering

This matters more than people realize. CS at UofA is in the **Faculty of Science**, not Engineering. This means:

- You're not an Engineering student, and your degree is a **Bachelor of Science**.
- **Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE)** is in the Faculty of Engineering. If you're interested in hardware, embedded systems, or signals, ECE might be worth looking at, but it's a different program with a different culture and different requirements.

For most software engineering jobs, coming from the Faculty of Science is completely fine.

**CS does have an internship program.** The Faculty of Science runs the [Science Internship Program (SIP)](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/science/student-services/internship-careers/student-interns/index.html): paid 8-, 12-, or 16-month placements that are noted on your transcript and parchment. It is optional for most CS paths and mandatory for the Software Practice Option. Check the SIP site for eligibility rules and application windows. You can also land internships on your own outside SIP; plenty of students do both.

## Minor Options Worth Considering

Your program leaves room for a minor, and the right minor can genuinely differentiate you. Here are the options worth thinking about:

**Statistics:** If you have any interest in machine learning, data science, or AI, a Stats minor is extremely practical. Your CS program already requires intro stats, and Honors and both Options add a second stats course (STAT 252 or 266); upper-year Stats courses on inference and probability complement ML coursework directly. Highly recommended.

**Mathematics:** Signals strong theoretical foundations. Relevant if you're interested in cryptography, algorithms research, or competitive programming. Linear algebra (MATH 125 or 127) is already part of your CS requirements; a Math minor builds on it.

**Business (through the Alberta School of Business):** Useful if you're thinking about eventually founding something, going into product management, or working at the intersection of tech and business. Some CS students find the business perspective grounding. The coursework isn't technically challenging, which gives your GPA a bit of breathing room.

**Linguistics:** Underrated. If you're interested in natural language processing, computational linguistics, or the theory of language and computation, UofA's Linguistics department is actually quite strong. This is a niche pick but a smart one if it aligns with your interests.

You do not need a minor. If taking one means overloading yourself and tanking your GPA, skip it.

## What Recruiters Actually Look At

Let's be direct about this, because too many students optimize for the wrong things.

**Internship experience** is the most important signal for early-career hiring, by a significant margin. One strong internship at a real company is worth more than any combination of degree type, GPA, and coursework. Two or three internships and you're essentially employable before you graduate.

**Projects** are your portfolio when you don't have internships yet. Recruiters at technical companies look at GitHub. Build things. Put them online. Make them work.

**GPA** matters, but not in the way students think. A GPA below ~3.0 can get you screened out at companies that use GPA cutoffs (some larger companies do). Above 3.0, marginal GPA differences matter far less than your experience. A 3.9 with no internships will lose to a 3.3 with two solid internships almost every time.

**Degree type (Major vs Honors, or which option)** is not a major signal for industry hiring. Hiring managers at most companies are not scrutinizing your degree classification. They're looking at where you worked, what you built, and how you perform in their interview process.

---

## The Bottom Line

- **Going into industry?** The Major is enough. If you want structured industry experience baked in, look at the Software Practice Option, but know the internship is mandatory.
- **Know you want ML/AI?** The AI Option gives you the core AI sequence, at the cost of elective freedom.
- **Interested in research or grad school?** Seriously consider Honors. Talk to professors early.
- **Don't waste time optimizing your degree classification.** Optimize your internship pipeline and your portfolio instead.

The students who land strong jobs out of UofA are the ones who started building things in first year, got their first internship in second or third year, and treated the degree as a foundation rather than the goal itself.
