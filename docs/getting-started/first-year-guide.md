# First Year Guide

Your first year decides two things that are hard to undo: which intro sequence you're on, and whether 204 can start in your second year. This page covers both, plus where to get help.

---

## 174/175 or 274/275?

Facts from the catalogue ([174](https://apps.ualberta.ca/catalogue/course/cmput/174), [175](https://apps.ualberta.ca/catalogue/course/cmput/175), [274](https://apps.ualberta.ca/catalogue/course/cmput/274), [275](https://apps.ualberta.ca/catalogue/course/cmput/275)):

| | CMPUT 174 / 175 | CMPUT 274 / 275 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Introduction to the Foundations of Computation I/II | **Accelerated** Introduction to the Foundations of Computation I/II |
| Prior experience | "No prior programming experience is necessary" | "Python or prior computing background is **strongly recommended**" |
| Format | Lecture + lab | **Studio-style**: lecture and lab blended into 3-hour sessions, twice a week |
| Enrollment | Regular | **Limited** by the capacity of the studio facilities |
| Languages | Problem-driven, language-independent emphasis | 274: Python on Linux. 275: C++ and object-oriented programming |
| Terms | Both offered Fall, Winter, and Spring | 274 Fall only, 275 Winter only |
| Prerequisite | Math 30, 30-1, or 30-2 | Math 30-1 |

**Credit exclusions:** you can't get credit for 274 together with 174 or 175, or for 275 together with 175. You also **can't get credit for both 275 and 201**, and in most CS paths the calendar requires you to replace 201 with another CMPUT course at the 200 level or above ([calendar](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110935)). In return, 275 satisfies the "201 or 275" prerequisite on 229, 291, and 301, and the "201 and 204, or 275" prerequisite on 313, 325, and 379.

**Our recommendation:** if you've already written real programs (beyond a high school intro course) and want C++ and Linux early, take 274/275. Otherwise take 174/175; the catalogue is explicit that 274 assumes background, and a rough first term costs you GPA that matters for scholarships (below). Both paths reach 204 at the same time.

## First-Year Math

Take **Calculus I, linear algebra, and intro stats in first year**, and Calculus II by the end of it. Calculus I is a prerequisite for CMPUT 204 and 267, and linear algebra and stats gate 200, 261, 267, 304, 313, 325, and more. For a Major, the usual combo is **MATH 144 → 146, MATH 125, STAT 151**. Honors students can use MATH 117/118 and 127 instead.

Full details, including MATH 117 and why MATH 114 doesn't count: [Math and Stats](math-and-stats.md).

## Your First Term

A typical first Fall that keeps every path open (from the [sample plans](degree-planning.md#sample-plans)):

| Fall | Winter |
|---|---|
| CMPUT 174 (or 274) | CMPUT 175 (or 275) |
| MATH 144 (or 134/154/117) | **CMPUT 272** |
| MATH 125 (or 127) | MATH 146 (or 136/156/118) |
| STAT 151 | ENGL or WRS |
| ENGL or WRS | Breadth / lab science |

The key move is **CMPUT 272 in your first Winter**: it only needs 174 (or 274), and 204 needs it. If you leave 272 to second year, 204 and everything behind it slides back a term.

Register in [Bear Tracks](https://www.beartracks.ualberta.ca/); the department says new students can register after early June once they have their CCID ([Getting Started](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/computing-science/undergraduate-studies/getting-started.html)).

## Where to Get Help

- **Your lab instructor and TAs:** the department's [Getting Started page](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/computing-science/undergraduate-studies/getting-started.html) says to consult them during your lab or by email. You can also sit in other sections' labs, though enrolled students get seats first.
- **CS undergraduate advising:** [csugrad@ualberta.ca](mailto:csugrad@ualberta.ca), or book an appointment from the [Undergraduate Studies page](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/computing-science/undergraduate-studies/index.html). For program, prerequisite, and substitution questions.
- **CS accounts and lab machines:** [CS Technical Support](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/computing-science/resources/technical-support/index.html); campus-wide accounts go to IST.
- **UACS (Undergraduate Association of Computing Science):** the CS student association. Office L1-339 CCIS and a lounge in UCOMM 1-130 with rentable lockers ([uacs.ca](https://uacs.ca/)). The department notes they run lab intros at the start of the year and can connect you with a tutor ([student groups](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/computing-science/resources/student-groups.html)).
- **Ada's Team** (diversity in computing, games, and technology) and the **Programming Club** (contest practice) are also listed on the department's [student groups page](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/computing-science/resources/student-groups.html). More in [Clubs and Orgs](../community/clubs-and-orgs.md).

Habits, briefly: go to the labs, start assignments the day they're posted, and ask for help before you're stuck for a day rather than after.

## Grades and the Jason Lang Scholarship

If you're an Alberta resident, your first-year GPA decides whether you get the provincial **Jason Lang** (and Louise McKinney) scholarship in second year. The minimum GPA is now **3.5**, up from 3.2, with at least an 80% course load over Fall and Winter. Details and sources: [Scholarships](../resources/scholarships.md).

If you're thinking about Honors, note the bar there too: a **3.0 GPA every Fall/Winter** to continue ([BSc calendar](https://calendar.ualberta.ca/preview_program.php?catoid=69&poid=110969)).
